## Context

[00 §4](../00-product-vision.md) is explicit: "General ledger / double-entry accounting — the engine emits signals; a GL system consumes them." The GL system is out of scope as a product but the signal boundary to it is in scope. [Q-AB](../04-open-questions.md) frames the open work: "the engine emits raw business events; the GL system needs a small adapter to consume them and produce postings — adapter shape, ownership, and consumption contract are coordination work with the GL team."

This is the boundary the contract crosses: the engine's outbox (every state-changing operation produces a domain event, per [01 §6](../01-product-architecture.md) and [ADR-IC-004](../../integration_concepts/adrs/ADR-IC-004-outbox-pattern-mechanism.md)) on one side; the GL system's double-entry journal on the other. The engine **already emits** a full business-event catalogue — `DepositConstituted`, `InterestAccrued`, `WithholdingApplied`, `InterestPaid`, `DepositMatured`, `DepositTerminatedEarly`, `DepositCorrected`, … ([02 §2.4](../02-v1-scope-term-deposits.md)) — over that outbox, governed by the event catalogue ([integration_concepts §08](../../integration_concepts/08-event-catalog-governance.md)) and the schema registry ([ADR-IC-002](../../integration_concepts/adrs/ADR-IC-002-schema-format-and-registry.md)). So the decision is **not** "what new event does the engine emit for the GL"; it is which of three shapes the boundary takes, and the ownership / idempotency / period-close / reconciliation commitments that follow:

- **(a)** The engine emits raw business events; a GL-side adapter translates each into postings.
- **(b)** The engine emits GL-aware postings (debits/credits against a chart of accounts) directly.
- **(c)** Both — raw events plus an opt-in engine-shipped posting projection.

Shape (b) drags the bank's chart of accounts, its double-entry rules, and its period-close policy into the engine — exactly the out-of-scope absorption [00 §4](../00-product-vision.md) forbids, and a coupling the swappable-pack thesis cannot carry (the chart of accounts is operating-bank-specific, not pack-specific). Shape (c) buys nothing shape (a) lacks while making the engine co-own accounting semantics it has no authority over. The brief already names shape (a); this ADR fixes it as the contract and fills the six slots.

## Decision

The engine emits **raw business events**; a **GL-side posting adapter, owned by the GL team**, translates them into double-entry postings. The engine never emits a posting and never holds a chart of accounts.

1. **Payload shape.** The contract payload is the **existing [02 §2.4](../02-v1-scope-term-deposits.md) business-event catalogue and envelope** — referenced, not redefined. No GL-specific event type is introduced. The GL adapter subscribes to the accounting-relevant subset (constitution, accrual, withholding, interest payment, maturity, early termination, partial withdrawal, correction, transfer-to-heirs) on the bank's event backbone ([ADR-IC-001](../../integration_concepts/adrs/ADR-IC-001-event-backbone-message-broker.md)). Each event already carries integer-cents amounts, `EUR` currency, value-date (`valid_time`), `instance_id`, and the `event_id` / `causation_id` / `correlation_id` triad ([02 §2.4.3](../02-v1-scope-term-deposits.md)) — everything a posting set needs. PII is not on the accounting path; the GL adapter consumes structural fields only, so [ADR-PC-004](./ADR-PC-004-pii-crypto-shredding.md) crypto-shredding never strands a posting.

2. **Semantics.** Each business event asserts a *business fact* ("net interest of N cents was paid to current account X on date D"). The GL adapter maps that fact to a posting set under a **chart-of-accounts translation table the GL team owns** — e.g. `DepositConstituted` → Dr customer current account / Cr deposit liability; `InterestAccrued` → Dr interest expense / Cr accrued-interest payable; `WithholdingApplied` → Dr accrued-interest payable / Cr tax-authority payable; `InterestPaid` → settle accrued against the current account. The engine commits to the *meaning and arithmetic of the fact*; the GL system owns the *accounts and the double-entry rule*. The engine has no opinion on the chart of accounts and never sees one.

3. **Ordering and delivery guarantees.** **At-least-once**, via the outbox relay ([ADR-IC-004](../../integration_concepts/adrs/ADR-IC-004-outbox-pattern-mechanism.md)) to the backbone. **Per-instance order is preserved** (`partition_key = instance_id`, [02 §2.4.3](../02-v1-scope-term-deposits.md)); there is **no cross-instance global order** — the GL adapter must not assume a global sequence across deposits. Gap detection is the GL adapter's responsibility and is backstopped by the reconciliation contract (slot 6 / Consequences): the engine outbox is the authoritative emitted-fact record against which the GL journal is reconciled.

4. **Idempotency.** The idempotency key is **`event_id`** (envelope, [02 §2.4.3](../02-v1-scope-term-deposits.md)). Each posting set is keyed by the `event_id` of the fact that produced it, so a redelivered or replayed event yields **no duplicate journal entry** — the adapter dedupes on `event_id`. Because events are immutable and retained for the life of the instance, the key is unique forever; the adapter's dedupe horizon must match its own replay/catch-up window, not a short TTL. Dedupe runs on the **GL (consumer) side** — the engine makes no exactly-once promise beyond effectively-once outbox relay.

5. **Error model.** **Post-flag, never gated.** GL consumption is strictly downstream of the engine's local commit; a GL rejection (bad mapping, unavailable ledger, unbalanced posting) **must never block or compensate the producing business flow**. A constituted deposit is a committed business fact — it is not unwound because the GL adapter choked. GL-side failures are GL-side operational alerts plus reconciliation mismatches, resolved by **replay from the engine outbox**, which is always available because the events are the source of truth. This is the decisive consequence of "emit signals, consume downstream" and the load-bearing reason shape (b) is rejected: shape (b) would tempt a synchronous posting gate on the write path.

6. **Ownership and versioning.** The **engine owns** the business-event schemas (already governed under [integration_concepts §08](../../integration_concepts/08-event-catalog-governance.md) / [§09](../../integration_concepts/09-long-term-schema-evolution.md) and the [ADR-IC-002](../../integration_concepts/adrs/ADR-IC-002-schema-format-and-registry.md) registry). The **GL team owns** the adapter and the chart-of-accounts translation table. Breaking changes to a consumed event follow [§09](../../integration_concepts/09-long-term-schema-evolution.md): keep the name for backward-compatible evolution, publish a parallel `V2` event with a sunset window for incompatible change. A **consumer-driven contract test** (Pact, per [ADR-IC-009](../../integration_concepts/adrs/ADR-IC-009-testing-infrastructure.md)) lets the GL team pin the fields its postings depend on, so the engine learns at CI time — not in production — if a change breaks GL.

### Period-close against the bitemporal model

The engine **never mutates a past event**. A retroactive correction is a *new* event — `DepositCorrected` ([02 §2.4](../02-v1-scope-term-deposits.md)) — with a later `transaction_time` and the `valid_time` the correction applies to. The GL adapter receives the correction as a new fact and books it under the **GL team's prior-period-adjustment policy**: if the correction's `valid_time` falls in a GL period already closed, the GL system posts an adjustment in its current open period (or a formal prior-period restatement) per its own accounting rules. The engine supplies the bitemporal facts (what we knew, when, and what value-date they bear); it **does not reach into closed GL periods** and holds no notion of GL period boundaries. This keeps the two systems' close cycles independent: the GL closes on its calendar, the engine keeps emitting immutable facts and corrections.

## Consequences

**Easier.** The engine stays a product engine, not an accounting engine — no chart of accounts, no double-entry rules, no period-close logic crosses the boundary, honouring [00 §4](../00-product-vision.md). The GL system can be swapped, re-mapped, or re-charted with **zero engine change**, because it consumes business facts, not postings. Replay-from-outbox gives GL a free, authoritative recovery and back-fill path. The contract reuses the existing catalogue, envelope, outbox, registry, and catalogue-governance machinery — no new transport, no new schema family.

**Harder / locked-in.** The GL team must build and own a translation table and an idempotent, replay-tolerant adapter — real work that this contract pushes onto the counterparty by design. The engine commits to the **stability of the business-event catalogue as public API**: once a GL posting depends on a field, that field is contract-governed and can only change through the [§09](../../integration_concepts/09-long-term-schema-evolution.md) deprecation path. Reconciliation is now a standing operational duty: a daily compare of the **engine outbox (emitted facts) against the GL incoming journal (posted entries)**, with mismatches raised as operational alerts on the same threshold machinery as [Q-AG](../04-open-questions.md) and the legacy-settlement reconciliation in [02 §3](../02-v1-scope-term-deposits.md) — not silent drift.

**Impossible by construction.** The engine cannot emit an unbalanced or chart-aware posting (it emits none). A GL outage cannot stall or fail a deposit operation (post-flag, never gated). A retroactive correction cannot silently rewrite a closed GL period (corrections are new immutable facts the GL books under its own policy).

## Residual risks

- **What this contract does not commit to.** The chart-of-accounts translation table itself, the GL system's prior-period-adjustment accounting policy, and the reconciliation alert thresholds are **GL-team / operating-bank deliverables**, not engine deliverables. This ADR fixes the *shape* (raw events, GL-side adapter, post-flag, `event_id` idempotency, reconcile-against-outbox); the *mapping content* lands in a GL-team coordination that is a **production gate, not a POC prerequisite** — the same accept-now-gate-at-production posture as [ADR-PC-004](./ADR-PC-004-pii-crypto-shredding.md) and [ADR-PC-005](./ADR-PC-005-dr-rto-rpo.md).
- **Reconciliation calibration is deferred.** "How many mismatches per day cross from noise to incident" is [Q-AG](../04-open-questions.md), requiring a calibration period under real load. Until then the reconciliation exists but its thresholds are provisional.
- **Failure modes the contract permits.** A GL adapter that under-subscribes (misses an accounting-relevant event type as the catalogue grows in v2+) will under-post until reconciliation catches it; the Pact contract narrows but does not eliminate this — a *newly added* event type is invisible to an existing consumer contract. The mitigation is catalogue-governance review ([integration_concepts §08](../../integration_concepts/08-event-catalog-governance.md)) flagging new accounting-relevant events to the GL team at authoring time.
- **GL-aware events were considered for one case and rejected.** Tax remittance (`WithholdingApplied` → tax-authority payable) is the event most tempting to emit GL-aware, because the posting is near-universal. It is still emitted raw: a PT-specific tax-payable account is no less operating-bank-specific than any other account, and carving one exception would erode the clean boundary for marginal benefit.
