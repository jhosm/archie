## Context

Pack-shipped disclosure templates — FIN, SECCI, FINE, maturity notices, annual IRS-withholding statements — are specified as pack vocabulary in [surface §3.3–§3.4](../feature-design-configuration-surface.md) (`disclosure.*`: v1 activates `fin`, the *Ficha de Informação Normalizada* for deposits; `fipre`/`fine` reserved). The templates exist; the **trigger and delivery contract does not**. [Q-AV](../04-open-questions.md) names the gap and the candidates: engine emits `NotificationDue` events per template-ref and a separate system renders and delivers; pack-side renderer with channel-side delivery; channel-side both. The choice "determines whether the engine carries a notification-state projection (sent/acked/bounced) or treats notifications as fire-and-forget."

v1 must deliver **FIN** for *depósito a prazo* ([02 §2.2](../02-v1-scope-term-deposits.md)): "The engine produces the data (rate, term, fees if any, TAE, indicative net return, deposit-guarantee statement); rendering and customer-facing distribution are in channels." That sentence already fixes the rendering/delivery split — the engine is a data source, not a renderer. What is undecided is the *trigger event shape*, whether the engine holds delivery state, the acknowledgement contract, and PII handling for templates that interpolate encrypted fields (coordinates with [ADR-PC-004](./ADR-PC-004-pii-crypto-shredding.md)).

The complication that shapes the whole contract: notifications fall into **three trigger kinds**, and they are not the same beast.

- **Event-driven** — a domain event implies a notification (e.g. `DepositMatured` → maturity notice). Naturally async, post-hoc.
- **Scheduled** — a temporal point implies it (e.g. the 14-day pre-maturity opt-out window, [02 §2.4.4](../02-v1-scope-term-deposits.md); the annual IRS-withholding statement aggregating `WithholdingApplied`). Also async.
- **Pre-contractual** — FIN. EU/BdP retail-conduct rules require the information sheet to **precede contract conclusion**. This one is *not* fire-and-forget after the fact: it is a synchronous gate before the customer commits.

A single fire-and-forget async emit is right for the first two and wrong for the third. The decision keeps the engine's emit path uniform and carves the pre-contractual gate out to where it already lives — the constitution saga.

## Decision

The engine emits a **`NotificationDue`** event (engine-cross-cutting, peer to the [02 §2.4.2](../02-v1-scope-term-deposits.md) generic events) when a notification becomes due; a **separate customer-communications system renders and delivers** it. The engine **carries no notification-state projection** — no `sent` / `acked` / `bounced`. From the engine's perspective notifications are fire-and-forget. The pre-contractual FIN gate is **not** carried by this emit path; it is a step in the constitution saga.

1. **Payload shape.** A new engine-cross-cutting event:

   ```
   NotificationDue
     notification_id,            -- idempotency key (slot 4)
     instance_id, customer_id,   -- customer_id is the recipient *reference* — see PII rule below
     template_ref,               -- pack-namespaced, e.g. pt.disclosure.fin, pt.notice.maturity
     template_pack_version,      -- the pack version pinned on the instance (ADR-PC-007)
     trigger_kind ∈ {EVENT_DRIVEN, SCHEDULED, PRE_CONTRACTUAL},
     causation_id,               -- the domain event (EVENT_DRIVEN) or schedule occurrence
     data: { structural interpolation values only — amounts, dates, rates,
             TAE; NO PII, see PII rule below },
     due_at                      -- = valid_time
   ```

   It wraps the standard envelope ([02 §2.4.3](../02-v1-scope-term-deposits.md)) — `event_id`, `partition_key = instance_id`, `valid_time`/`transaction_time`, `correlation_id`. The engine carries `customer_id` as a **reference** for *both* the delivery address (resolved channel-side, as before) *and* the template-body PII (resolved against the engine, see PII rule). **No PII — cleartext or ciphertext — rides this event.** The engine never holds the delivery address; the durable bus never carries the subject's name, NIF, or contact data.

2. **Semantics.** The event asserts "a notification of template `template_ref` is due for this instance, with this data." The receiver renders the named template (a pack artefact, [surface §3.4](../feature-design-configuration-surface.md) / [ADR-PC-007](./ADR-PC-007-signed-yaml-oci-pack.md)) against `data` and delivers it over the customer's preferred channel. The engine commits to *which template, with what values, on what date*; the communications system owns *rendering, channel selection, delivery, retry*. The template referenced is the version **pinned on the instance** (`template_pack_version`), so a deposit constituted under `pt.2026.1` keeps disclosing under that pack's templates even after `pt.2027.1` ships — the same pinning invariant as every other pack primitive ([01 §5](../01-product-architecture.md), [surface §3.5](../feature-design-configuration-surface.md)).

3. **Ordering and delivery guarantees.** **At-least-once**, via the outbox ([ADR-IC-004](../../integration_concepts/adrs/ADR-IC-004-outbox-pattern-mechanism.md)). **Per-instance order** (`partition_key = instance_id`); no cross-instance order. A redelivered or replayed `NotificationDue` is the expected case the receiver must absorb without re-sending (slot 4), not an error.

4. **Idempotency.** The key is **`notification_id`**, a composite of `instance_id` + `template_ref` + the triggering occurrence (`causation_id` for EVENT_DRIVEN; the schedule-occurrence identifier for SCHEDULED). It is stable across outbox redelivery **and across event-store replay** — replaying the log re-derives the same `notification_id`, so a rebuild does not re-notify the customer. Dedupe runs on the **communications-system (consumer) side**: it owns the "already delivered this one" record, because it owns delivery. This is the structural reason the engine does *not* need a delivery-state projection.

5. **Error model.** **Post-flag, never gated** for EVENT_DRIVEN and SCHEDULED. Delivery is downstream of the engine's commit; a render or delivery failure (bad address, channel down, bounce) **never blocks or compensates the producing flow** — a matured deposit is matured whether or not its maturity notice was delivered. Bounce / retry / dead-letter is the communications system's lifecycle. **The PRE_CONTRACTUAL exception is carved out of this path entirely** (see below): FIN's legal "must precede conclusion" gate is enforced *synchronously inside the constitution saga*, not by reacting to an async `NotificationDue`.

6. **Ownership and versioning.** The **engine owns** the `NotificationDue` schema and the `trigger_kind` taxonomy. The **pack owns** the templates and the `template_ref` namespace ([ADR-PC-007](./ADR-PC-007-signed-yaml-oci-pack.md)). The **communications system owns** rendering, delivery, and delivery state. Breaking changes to `NotificationDue` follow [integration_concepts §09](../../integration_concepts/09-long-term-schema-evolution.md) (parallel `V2`, sunset window); a consumer-driven contract test (Pact, [ADR-IC-009](../../integration_concepts/adrs/ADR-IC-009-testing-infrastructure.md)) pins the fields the renderer depends on.

### PII by reference, resolved at render time — coordinates with ADR-PC-004

Templates interpolate PII (customer name, NIF, address on a maturity notice or withholding statement), but **the PII does not travel on the event**. The `data` payload carries **structural values only** (amounts, dates, rates, TAE, `template_ref`). The communications system resolves the PII it needs at render time by calling an **engine-owned, authorised PII-resolve surface** with the `customer_id` / `subject_id` reference — e.g. `GET /v1/pii/resolve?subject=<ref>&fields=[name,nif]`. The engine decrypts internally via OpenBao transit ([ADR-PC-004 §P2](./ADR-PC-004-pii-crypto-shredding.md): "PII decryption happens only when PII is actually surfaced — display, regulatory report") and returns plaintext, or **null for a crypto-shredded subject** ([ADR-PC-004 §P3](./ADR-PC-004-pii-crypto-shredding.md)).

This keeps the **OpenBao boundary entirely inside the engine** — the communications system holds no key material and needs no crypto knowledge — and keeps **plaintext PII off the durable backbone entirely**. Shipping plaintext PII on the bus was rejected because it breaks crypto-shredding at the boundary: the broker ([ADR-IC-001](../../integration_concepts/adrs/ADR-IC-001-event-backbone-message-broker.md)) retains it past key destruction and every consumer that persists it becomes an independent PII store, recreating the GDPR Article 17 distributed-delete problem [ADR-PC-004](./ADR-PC-004-pii-crypto-shredding.md) was adopted to eliminate. Shipping PII *ciphertext* on the bus (a viable alternative — it shreds with the key like the event log) was rejected for this contract because it pushes OpenBao decrypt access out to every consumer; resolve-by-reference keeps that boundary internal. PII therefore materialises **transiently at the consumer, at the moment of use, fetched on demand** — never on a durable medium outside the engine. The reference pattern is the same one this contract already uses for the delivery address; applying it to body PII keeps the contract internally consistent. (This is a cross-cutting PII-on-the-bus principle — IFRS 9 ([ADR-PC-015](./ADR-PC-015-ifrs9-signal-contract.md), which inherits this rule by reference) and the reporting contracts ([Q-AX](../04-open-questions.md)) hit it too; it may warrant promotion to an [ADR-PC-004](./ADR-PC-004-pii-crypto-shredding.md) amendment so each contract need not restate it.)

### FIN is a saga step, not a fire-and-forget emit

FIN is **pre-contractual**: BdP retail-conduct rules require the customer to receive the information sheet **before** the deposit is concluded. That gate lives in the **constitution saga** ([integration_concepts §05](../../integration_concepts/05-constitution-saga-walkthrough.md), [ADR-IC-003](../../integration_concepts/adrs/ADR-IC-003-saga-orchestrator.md)): the engine produces the FIN *data* during the saga ([02 §2.2](../02-v1-scope-term-deposits.md)); the channel renders it, presents it, and **captures the customer's acknowledgement before the saga commits the constitution**. A missing or un-acknowledged FIN **fails the saga** (a `DepositConstitutionFailed`, [02 §2.4](../02-v1-scope-term-deposits.md), reason `COMPLIANCE_REJECTED`) — that is the gating semantics FIN's legal status demands. The engine *may* additionally emit a `NotificationDue(template_ref=pt.disclosure.fin, trigger_kind=PRE_CONTRACTUAL)` as an audit/record copy after the saga commits, but the **legally load-bearing pre-contractual gate is the synchronous saga step**, not that event. This is why `trigger_kind` carries `PRE_CONTRACTUAL` as a distinct value: it marks notifications whose legal force was discharged synchronously elsewhere, so no downstream consumer mistakes the record copy for the gate.

## Consequences

**Easier.** The engine stays out of the communications business — no contact directory, no channel logic, no retry/bounce state, no `sent`/`acked`/`bounced` projection to keep consistent. The communications system can be swapped or re-platformed with **zero engine change**, and needs **no OpenBao access or crypto knowledge** — it fetches the PII it needs by reference. Templates evolve as pack artefacts on the pack cadence, pinned per instance for free. The contract reuses the existing envelope, outbox, and registry — same spine as the GL contract ([ADR-PC-012](./ADR-PC-012-gl-posting-signal-contract.md)). Crypto-shredding composes cleanly and the broker never becomes a PII store: erased subjects' notifications resolve to null PII automatically, and no GDPR retention-bounding of topics is required.

**Harder / locked-in.** The communications system must own idempotent delivery keyed on `notification_id`, and rendering now depends on a **live call to the engine's PII-resolve surface** — so a batch statement run cannot render fully offline; it is coupled to engine availability at render time (acceptable for latency-tolerant notifications, but a real dependency). The engine commits *both* `NotificationDue` and the PII-resolve surface as **public API** governed by [§09](../../integration_concepts/09-long-term-schema-evolution.md). The FIN carve-out means **two paths** for one template family (synchronous saga gate + optional async record copy); the `trigger_kind` discriminator is the seam that keeps them from being confused, but it is a seam a reader has to understand.

**Impossible by construction.** A delivery failure cannot stall or fail a domain operation (post-flag for EVENT_DRIVEN/SCHEDULED). A replay or redelivery cannot double-notify a customer (`notification_id` idempotency). Plaintext PII cannot land on the durable backbone (it is never emitted), and a crypto-shredded subject's notification cannot resurrect their PII (resolve returns null). The engine cannot become the system of record for "was the customer told" — because it deliberately holds no delivery state.

## Residual risks

- **No engine-side proof of delivery.** The deliberate cost of carrying no notification-state projection: the engine cannot answer "was the maturity notice delivered, and when." For v1 this is acceptable — proof-of-delivery for the one *legally* load-bearing disclosure (FIN) lives with the **saga acknowledgement** captured at constitution, and post-hoc notices are advisory. If a later regulator or product requires the **engine** to hold auditable proof-of-delivery for a post-hoc disclosure (plausible for credit dunning / SECCI re-disclosure, [Q-AS](../04-open-questions.md)), this contract is **reopened** — a `NotificationDelivered` acknowledgement event flowing back from the communications system, and a delivery-state projection, would be the v-next extension. Named here so the reopen is a tracked decision, not a surprise.
- **PII-resolve availability is now on the render path.** Choosing resolve-by-reference (over self-contained ciphertext) trades a coupling: the communications system cannot render a PII-bearing notice while the engine's PII-resolve surface is down, so an overnight statement batch inherits the engine's availability. Mitigation is the latency-tolerance of notifications (retry the render later) plus the resolve surface riding the engine's own DR posture ([ADR-PC-005](./ADR-PC-005-dr-rto-rpo.md)); if a future consumer needs offline rendering, the self-contained-ciphertext variant (rejected here) is the documented fallback. The PII-resolve surface itself (field-level authorisation, audit of who resolved which subject's PII when) is an engine deliverable not fully specified by this contract.
- **What this contract does not commit to.** Channel selection and preference logic, the contact directory, retry/bounce policy, and the rendering engine are **communications-system deliverables**. The acknowledgement contract for the *pre-contractual* FIN gate (what evidence the channel captures, where it is stored) is a **saga-design / operating-bank deliverable** coordinated through [integration_concepts §05](../../integration_concepts/05-constitution-saga-walkthrough.md) — a production gate, not a POC prerequisite, matching the posture of [ADR-PC-004](./ADR-PC-004-pii-crypto-shredding.md).
- **Scheduled-trigger machinery is unspecified here.** *That* a SCHEDULED `NotificationDue` fires at a temporal point is contracted; *how* the engine's scheduler materialises temporal triggers (the pre-maturity window, the annual statement run) is engine-internal mechanism, out of this boundary contract. *Revised 2026-06-03 (Amendment A1): resolved by [ADR-PC-023](./ADR-PC-023-temporal-signals-projection-derived.md) — there is **no engine scheduler**; temporal triggers are downstream reads over the maturity-calendar / accrual-schedule / withholding-ledger projections.*
- **Failure mode the contract permits.** A SCHEDULED notification whose underlying data is later corrected (`DepositCorrected` after a maturity notice was emitted) can leave the customer holding a now-stale notice; the engine emits a fresh `NotificationDue` on the correction, but the superseding/withdrawal of the prior delivered notice is the communications system's concern, not the engine's.
