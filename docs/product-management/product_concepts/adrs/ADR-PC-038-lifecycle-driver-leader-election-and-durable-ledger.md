## Context

[ADR-PC-036](./ADR-PC-036-lifecycle-command-driver.md) built a downstream, clockless-engine-honoring **lifecycle-command driver** — a sibling host that owns the clock, ticks on a cadence, reads a forward calendar projection, and POSTs the engine's [ADR-PC-029](./ADR-PC-029-engine-command-ingress.md) maturity / installment command for each due occurrence. The skeleton (bd `babelstone-6cpq.7`) is built and **correct**, but not yet **production-operable**, and ADR-PC-036 said so explicitly: §Consequences names "a new deployable (single-firing/leader-election, monitoring, on-call)" and §Residual-risks parks the operating concerns as "not committed here." Epic `babelstone-1nkm` owns that hardening; this ADR is its **gating mechanism decision**, recorded before the two build siblings (single-firing, durable ledger) so the choice is governed, not silently improvised ([ADR-PC-020 §D3](./ADR-PC-020-llm-toolchain-and-conformance-governance.md)).

Two coupled gaps remain in the un-hardened host:

- **No cross-replica coordination (bd `babelstone-1nkm.2`).** A multi-replica deployment runs N independent tickers; each replica's `LifecycleSchedulePass` reads the same forward calendar, finds the same due occurrence, and POSTs it — **N redundant POSTs** for one occurrence.
- **An in-memory dispatch ledger (bd `babelstone-1nkm.3`).** `LifecycleDispatchLedger` is a `HashSet<Guid>` behind a lock. It does **not** survive a restart and is **not** shared across replicas: after a reboot the empty ledger re-derives and re-POSTs every still-due occurrence, and two replicas never see each other's dispatches.

**The correctness floor is already in place and is unchanged by this decision.** The engine's `command_dedup` ([ADR-PC-029 slot 4](./ADR-PC-029-engine-command-ingress.md), `ENGINE_COMMAND_IDEMPOTENT`), reached via the **number-pinned, server-derived** idempotency key `LifecycleCommandKey.Derive(instance_id, command_kind, occurrence)` (`LIFECYCLE_COMMAND_NUMBER_PINNED_IDEMPOTENT`, LCD-1, **Live**), collapses every redundant or restart-replayed POST to **one** money leg. So the un-hardened host is **safe today**, just wasteful and racy. This decision adds **efficiency, durability, and an audit trail** — never correctness, which stays the engine's.

**The repo already solves "N pods, each due unit handled exactly once."** It does so everywhere with one house pattern: a Postgres claim under `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, plus a per-key transaction-scoped advisory lock (`pg_try_advisory_xact_lock(hashtextextended(key, salt))`) where per-key ordering matters:

- The **orchestrator saga command dispatcher** (`orchestrator/src/Babelstone.Orchestrator.Substrate/Dispatch/SagaCommandDispatchDrainer.cs`, [ADR-PC-029 slot 3](./ADR-PC-029-engine-command-ingress.md)) claims each `saga_outbox` row `FOR UPDATE OF o SKIP LOCKED` under a per-`process_id` advisory lock, runs the HTTP call, and commits the status flip in the **same transaction** — a crash between the engine's 2xx and the commit leaves the row PENDING for an idempotent re-POST.
- The **engine outbox relay** (`engine/src/Babelstone.OutboxPublisher/OutboxDrainer.cs`, [ADR-IC-004 §P2](../../integration_concepts/adrs/ADR-IC-004-outbox-pattern-mechanism.md)) claims each `outbox` row `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` — so competing relay instances claim **disjoint** rows and never double-publish — under a per-`aggregate_id` advisory lock that additionally holds per-aggregate publish order.
- The **bulk-operations runner** ([ADR-PC-035 §P2](./ADR-PC-035-bulk-operations-execution-pattern.md), `BULK-1`) is *decided* to drain its work-table in `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` batches the same way — a third instance of the pattern, but Accepted-and-`Planned` (`BULK_OP_REGISTER_DRAIN_COMPLETE`), not yet built.

Crucially, **the repo has no single-leader-election primitive at all today** — every coordination point is *competing-consumers*: all instances run, each atomically claims a disjoint unit of work, and the durable claim (not an elected leader) is what makes each unit fire once. The advisory locks are transaction-scoped session state, never a singleton lease (migration `0007_saga_outbox_fifo_guard.sql` records this: "`pg_try_advisory_xact_lock` is session/transaction state, not a table"). Choosing a *dedicated leader* for the lifecycle host would therefore be a **divergence** from the established posture — allowed under [ADR-PC-020 §D3](./ADR-PC-020-llm-toolchain-and-conformance-governance.md), but only consciously and recorded. This ADR makes that comparison the centre of the decision.

No tool is purchased or licensed — every option is in-house .NET over the Postgres already in the stack, or a new dependency to operate. This is the [ADR-PC-000 §D3](./ADR-PC-000-namespace-and-contract-shape-framework.md) residual category: F1/F2 degenerate; the decision rides on S1–S4.

Four candidates were designed and adversarially reviewed. Each pairs a **single-firing mechanism** with a **durable-ledger substrate**:

| Candidate | Single-firing mechanism | Durable-ledger substrate |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Per-occurrence atomic claim** — every replica ticks; each due occurrence is claimed once with the existing `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` (+ per-instance `pg_try_advisory_xact_lock`) competing-consumers pattern; the winner POSTs it | A new **Postgres** `lifecycle_dispatch_ledger` table (the house default). The claim *and* the durable record are the **same** table — the two axes collapse into one mechanism |
| **B** | **Dedicated single-leader election** — one replica holds a session-scoped `pg_advisory_lock` on a fixed key (auto-released when its session drops) and is the sole ticker; standbys block/poll and take over on release | A separate Postgres dispatch-ledger table (still required — leader-election alone gives no crash-survival of "what did I already POST") |
| **C** | **External coordination service** — a K8s `Lease`, etcd, Consul, or ZooKeeper elects the leader | A Postgres ledger (or the coordinator's KV store) |
| **D** | **Redis-backed claim/lock** — `SET key NX EX` / Redlock both elects the firer and records the dispatch | **Redis** (an eviction-prone in-memory store, not a durable system-of-record) |

## Evaluation

### Hard filter results

#### F1 · Cost / licensing
| Candidate | Licence / cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A, B | In-house .NET over the Postgres already provisioned for the engine / saga / read-model stores; no new licence or runtime | Pass (degenerate) |
| C, D | etcd / Consul / ZooKeeper / Redis and the K8s Lease API are open-source / already-present — no licence fee. The cost is **operational**, not licensing, so it lands in S1, not here | Pass (degenerate) |

#### F2 · Regulatory fit (GDPR / DORA / PSD2)
| Candidate | Note | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A–D | No candidate moves PII anywhere: a dispatch-ledger row carries only structural references — an instance id, a command kind, a number-pinned occurrence key, a dispatched-at timestamp ([ADR-PC-004 §P2](./ADR-PC-004-pii-crypto-shredding.md)). All reach the bank's Core only through the existing [ADR-PC-032](./ADR-PC-032-money-movement-primitive.md) gated settlement leg | Pass |

The hard filters do not separate the candidates. The decision is **S1–S4** plus the decisive reason.

### Soft criteria

#### A (per-occurrence claim on a durable Postgres ledger) — CHOSEN
- **S1 · Operational complexity (1–2 people).** Lowest novelty by a wide margin: it is the **exact** pattern already running in the saga dispatcher and the outbox relay — and decided for the bulk runner ([ADR-PC-035](./ADR-PC-035-bulk-operations-execution-pattern.md), Accepted but not yet built) — `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` + a per-instance advisory lock + claim→act→commit. No new coordination concept enters the estate. There is **no leader to elect, no lease to renew, no heartbeat, no failover detector, no split-brain question**: every replica is always live, and a dead replica simply means fewer claimants while the survivors still fire every occurrence. The two build siblings collapse into one artefact — a single migration plus a claim query.
- **S2 · Ecosystem coherence.** It keeps the host inside the Postgres-centric, hand-rolled posture the whole estate uses for durable coordination ([ADR-PC-001](./ADR-PC-001-event-store-technology.md); the "hand-roll the core" preference). The claim key is the **same** number-pinned dispatch id the engine `command_dedup` keys on (LCD-1), so the ledger's "have I fired this?" and the engine's "have I applied this?" agree on occurrence identity by construction. The two-phase **claim→POST→record** ordering preserves the deliberate fallible-sink discipline the in-memory ledger already documents (check-then-POST-then-record, never reserve-before-POST that would strand an occurrence on a transient failure): the claim is a lease held in the open transaction, the POST runs, and the dispatched-at record commits with the claim release — exactly the saga dispatcher's claim→call→flip, so a crash between the engine's 2xx and the commit leaves the occurrence **re-claimable** and `command_dedup` makes the re-POST effectively-once.
- **S3 · Exit cost.** One Postgres table in the driver host's own forward-only migration series; nothing external to migrate off. If the per-tick read fan-out is ever measured to bite, candidate B layers **on top** without discarding the ledger (the durable table is the substrate either way).
- **S4 · Longevity.** One mechanism serves both the recurring (installment) and one-shot (maturity) cases and scales to a third lifecycle (deposit interest coupons) with no coordination change. The durable table gives the **audit trail** bd `babelstone-1nkm.3` requires — a queryable "what did the driver dispatch and when" — that an elected-leader-plus-volatile-set would not.
- **Decisive reason:** the durable dispatch ledger and single-firing are **not two problems** — the atomic claim on the durable table *is* the single-firing guard, and the table's persistence *is* the crash-survival and audit trail. Reusing the estate's one proven competing-consumers pattern solves both build siblings with the lowest-novelty path that keeps `command_dedup` the unchanged correctness floor.

#### B (dedicated single-leader election) — rejected (reserved as a measured scale-up)
A session-scoped `pg_advisory_lock` on a fixed key gives a literal "one active ticker, standbys on hot standby." Rejected for three reasons. (1) It is a **brand-new coordination posture** the estate does not use anywhere — a conscious divergence ([ADR-PC-020 §D3](./ADR-PC-020-llm-toolchain-and-conformance-governance.md)) that buys nothing A lacks. (2) It introduces a **failover liveness gap**: when the leader process dies without a clean disconnect, the advisory lock releases only when Postgres notices the dropped backend (TCP keepalive / `tcp_user_timeout` lag — seconds to minutes), a window in which **nothing ticks**; A has no such gap because all replicas are always live. (3) It does **not** subsume the durable-ledger requirement — a leader still needs a crash-surviving record of what it already POSTed, so B builds **both** a leader lock **and** the very ledger A makes self-sufficient. Its one genuine virtue — only one replica reads the calendar per tick — matters only if per-tick read fan-out over a very large portfolio is measured to bite. **Reserved** as exactly that scale-up, layered on A's durable table (mirroring how [ADR-PC-036](./ADR-PC-036-lifecycle-command-driver.md) reserves its candidate C for a measured poll-cost problem).

#### C (external coordination service) — rejected
A K8s Lease / etcd / Consul / ZooKeeper elects the leader robustly, but at the cost of a **new external dependency and operational surface** the estate deliberately avoids: it widens the DORA-relevant resilience footprint, fails S1 (a new failure mode, a new thing to run and monitor) and S3 (coupling the driver's liveness to an orchestrator-or-quorum it otherwise needs nothing from), and runs against the hand-rolled, Postgres-centric house style. And like B it still needs the durable dispatch ledger. No advantage over A justifies the dependency.

#### D (Redis-backed claim/lock) — rejected
`SET NX EX` / Redlock can both elect the firer and hold the dispatch set, but Redis is an **eviction-prone in-memory cache, not the durable system-of-record** the audit trail and crash-survival demand — a `dispatched-at` ledger that an eviction or a cold restart can silently drop is no better than today's `HashSet`. It adds a new stateful dependency outside the Postgres substrate (against S2/S3), and Redlock's safety under partition is a debate A simply does not have. Reject.

## Decision

**Harden the lifecycle-command driver by replacing its in-memory dispatch ledger with a durable Postgres `lifecycle_dispatch_ledger` table, and achieve cross-replica single-firing by claiming each due occurrence on that table with the estate's existing `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` competing-consumers pattern — so the durable ledger's atomic claim *is* the single-firing mechanism, and the two hardening axes (bd `babelstone-1nkm.2` and `babelstone-1nkm.3`) collapse into one Postgres mechanism. No dedicated leader is elected.**

1. **Substrate — Postgres, the house default.** The dispatch ledger is a `lifecycle_dispatch_ledger` table in the lifecycle-driver host's own forward-only migration series (authored via the `new-store-migration` discipline), the same durable substrate as the event store ([ADR-PC-001](./ADR-PC-001-event-store-technology.md)), the saga substrate ([ADR-IC-003](../../integration_concepts/adrs/ADR-IC-003-saga-orchestrator.md)), and family read models. **Not** Redis, **not** the durable bus (the bus is events-only, [Primitive 1](../../integration_concepts/01-the-six-primitives.md) / [ADR-PC-029](./ADR-PC-029-engine-command-ingress.md)). The row is keyed on the number-pinned, server-derived dispatch id `LifecycleCommandKey.Derive(instance_id, command_kind, stable_occurrence_key)` (LCD-1) and carries a `dispatched_at` audit column. It is PII-free ([ADR-PC-004 §P2](./ADR-PC-004-pii-crypto-shredding.md)).

2. **Single-firing — per-occurrence atomic claim, reusing the precedent.** Every replica runs the `LifecycleSchedulePass`; for each due occurrence it claims the ledger row under `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` (with a per-instance `pg_try_advisory_xact_lock(hashtextextended(instance_id, salt))` where per-instance ordering of recurring occurrences matters), exactly as `SagaCommandDispatchDrainer` ([ADR-PC-029 slot 3](./ADR-PC-029-engine-command-ingress.md)) and `OutboxDrainer` ([ADR-IC-004 §P2](../../integration_concepts/adrs/ADR-IC-004-outbox-pattern-mechanism.md)) do. The replica that wins the claim POSTs the command; the others skip that occurrence this tick. There is **no elected leader** — single-firing emerges from the atomic claim on the shared durable table.

3. **Claim→POST→record, crash-safe by the precedent.** The claim is a lease held inside the open transaction; the fallible POST runs; the `dispatched_at` record commits with the claim release in the **same transaction**. A crash between the engine's 2xx and the commit leaves the occurrence **un-recorded and re-claimable**, and the engine's `command_dedup` (`ENGINE_COMMAND_IDEMPOTENT`) replays the original outcome on the re-POST — effectively-once, identical to the saga dispatcher's claim→call→flip. This preserves the existing `LifecycleDispatchLedger`'s deliberate two-phase ordering (never reserve-before-POST, which would strand an occurrence on a transient failure).

4. **The engine `command_dedup` stays the authoritative correctness floor.** The durable ledger is the front-line optimization that stops the driver re-POSTing every tick and across restarts; it is **never** the safety mechanism. A bug, gap, or race in the ledger degrades to *redundant POSTs the engine dedupes* — exactly today's safe-but-wasteful behaviour — never to a double money leg.

5. **Reject B, C, D; reserve B.** No dedicated single-leader election (B — a new coordination posture with a failover liveness gap that still needs the ledger; **reserved** as the measured scale-up if per-tick calendar-read fan-out over a large portfolio is shown to bite). No external coordination service (C — a new DORA-relevant dependency against the hand-rolled Postgres posture). No Redis substrate (D — an eviction-prone cache, not a durable system-of-record).

6. **Monitoring / on-call is a sibling concern, not decided here.** bd `babelstone-1nkm.4` owns the host's liveness, dispatch-lag, POST-failure, and parked-settlement alerting. This ADR governs the single-firing + durable-ledger mechanism only.

## Consequences

**Easier.** The driver inherits the estate's one tested, governed competing-consumers pattern — zero new coordination primitive, no leader-failover machinery, no lease/heartbeat/TTL, no split-brain question, no external dependency. Every replica is always live, so there is no "leader is dead but the lease has not expired" dark window. Both hardening build siblings ship as **one** migration plus **one** claim query. The audit trail bd `babelstone-1nkm.3` wants falls out of the durable table for free.

**Harder / locked-in.** A new driver-owned Postgres migration series and the `lifecycle_dispatch_ledger` table are real new artefacts (and a new migration discipline surface for the host). The in-memory `HashSet` membership check becomes a Postgres round-trip per due occurrence — negligible at lifecycle volumes (daily maturities, monthly installments), but no longer free. All N replicas read the forward calendar each tick (redundant reads; the claim dedupes the writes) — the cost that reserves candidate B.

**Impossible by construction.** Two replicas cannot both POST the same occurrence while the claim is atomic (`FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` hands the row to exactly one). A host restart cannot re-POST an already-dispatched occurrence — the durable row survives the reboot (the defect that motivated bd `babelstone-1nkm.3`). The engine still cannot be driven past `command_dedup` — the ledger sits in front of it, never around it. A clock still cannot enter the engine (the driver is downstream; BENG004 holds, [ADR-PC-023](./ADR-PC-023-temporal-signals-projection-derived.md)).

## Residual risks

- **Per-tick calendar-read fan-out grows with replica count × portfolio size.** All replicas read the forward calendar every tick; only the writes are deduped by the claim. Acceptable at v1 lifecycle volumes; the reserved candidate B (single-leader, one reader) is the scale-up path if it is ever measured to bite.
- **A permanently parked settlement still silently stalls a recurring schedule** (the [ADR-PC-036](./ADR-PC-036-lifecycle-command-driver.md) settlement-health gate holds occurrence N+1, `LIFECYCLE_DRIVER_SETTLEMENT_HEALTH_GATE`/LCD-2). It must be **alerted, not invisible** — there is no arrears state for the miss to land in. That alerting is bd `babelstone-1nkm.4`, **not** closed by this decision.
- **`command_dedup` retention must exceed the driver's longest retry/backfill window** (inherited from [ADR-PC-036](./ADR-PC-036-lifecycle-command-driver.md) §Residual-risks), since a repeated `PayInstallment` from `Active` has no legality backstop and rests entirely on the number-pinned key reaching `command_dedup`.
- **The advisory-lock salt must namespace the lifecycle host's key space** so a per-instance lock cannot collide with the saga dispatcher's or relay's advisory locks on a shared cluster (the same discipline `FifoLockSalt` / the outbox seed already follow). A build-time concern for bd `babelstone-1nkm.2`.
