## In plain English

Portugal's credit-card market has three kinds of players. The **big universal banks**
(Caixa Geral de Depósitos, Millennium BCP, Santander Totta, Novo Banco, BPI…) sell cards as
one product among many to their existing customers. The **card specialists / "monoliners"**
(WiZink, Unicre/Universo, Cofidis, Cetelem) do little *except* consumer credit, so their cards
push revolving balances and installments hardest — and one of them, the **Universo** card tied
to the Continente/Sonae retail empire, has an outsized base because shopping loyalty pulls
people to it. Then the **digital challengers** (ActivoBank, and especially **Revolut**) compete
on fees, FX, and app experience, dragging the incumbents' pricing and UX along.

Two market truths from Brief 02 shape all of this: pricing **clusters under the legal TAEG
cap**, and a lot of cards are used in **pay-in-full mode**, so issuers compete as much on
*rewards and loyalty* as on *interest*.

## 2. Player taxonomy

**Universal / incumbent banks**
- **Caixa Geral de Depósitos (CGD)** — state-owned, largest by reach; *Caixadirecta* app.
- **Millennium BCP** — largest private bank.
- **Santander Totta** — Santander group.
- **Novo Banco** — successor to BES.
- **BPI** — part of the CaixaBank group.
- **Bankinter**, **Crédito Agrícola**, **Abanca**, **Montepio**, **EuroBic** — mid/regional.

**Digital arms of incumbents**
- **ActivoBank** (BCP) — low-fee, younger-skewing digital bank.
- **Moey** (Crédito Agrícola), **Openbank** (Santander) — digital propositions.

**Card specialists / monoliners (consumer finance)**
- **WiZink Bank** — pure card/credit specialist (absorbed the former Barclaycard Portugal and
  Banco Popular card books); revolving cards with flexible payment.
- **Unicre / UNIBANCO** — *Instituição Financeira de Crédito*; issues the **Universo** co-brand
  card (with Sonae MC) and runs **REDUNIQ** acquiring.
- **Cofidis** — revolving cards + installments + personal loans.
- **Cetelem** (BNP Paribas Personal Finance) — consumer-finance cards & installments.
- **Oney**, **Younited** — consumer finance / BNPL-adjacent.

**Retail co-brand gravity**
- **Universo** (Sonae MC + Unicre) — the credit instrument inside the **Continente / Worten**
  ecosystem; large base driven by retail loyalty + installments. (The *Cartão Continente*
  itself is a loyalty card; Universo is the credit card in that family.)

**Digital / neobank / fintech & BNPL**
- **Revolut** — very high PT adoption; multi-currency, FX-friendly, strong app. `[REFRESH]`
  whether a true revolving **credit** card is live in PT vs. debit/charge-style products.
- **N26** — debit-focused.
- **BNPL:** Klarna, Cofidis, Oney — eroding the installment use case.

## 4. Cross-cutting themes

1. **Pricing converges toward the TAEG cap.** Because BdP caps revolving TAEG (Brief 02 §2),
   headline revolving rates cluster just under the ceiling — competition shifts to *rewards,
   fees, and loyalty* rather than rate.
2. **Deferred-debit dominance.** Many cards are used pay-in-full, so issuers earn
   **interchange + anuidade**, not interest — making **rewards/co-brand the battleground**.
3. **Continente/Universo loyalty gravity.** Retail ecosystem pulls card choice; hard for a
   pure-bank card to match the everyday-grocery value.
4. **Revolut disruption.** FX, virtual cards, and UX pressure incumbent fees and digital
   roadmaps, especially among younger users.
5. **BNPL encroachment.** Cofidis/Oney/Klarna eat the installment use case that PT cards
   historically served via *pagamento em prestações*.
6. **Stamp duty wedge.** *Imposto do selo* (Brief 02 §2) makes the *true* cost of revolving
   exceed the headline TAN — a point any honest cost comparison must surface.
7. **The domestic rail is mandatory.** MB WAY / Multibanco integration is table stakes; no
   issuer competes in PT without it.
