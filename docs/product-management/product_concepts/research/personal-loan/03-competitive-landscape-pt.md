## In plain English

Portugal's personal-loan market has two big tribes plus a digital fringe. The **universal banks**
(Caixa Geral de Depósitos, Millennium BCP, Santander Totta, Novo Banco, BPI…) sell crédito
pessoal as one product among many to their account holders, funded from their own deposits. The
**consumer-finance specialists** (Cofidis, Cetelem, Banco Credibom, Younited…) do little *except*
lend — so they compete hardest on speed, online origination, point-of-sale reach, and
consolidation, and they lean on **insurance attached to the loan** for margin. Around the edges, a
few **digital/fintech** players (Banco CTT, and possibly Revolut) push instant, app-first loans.

Two truths from Brief 02 shape everything: pricing **clusters just under the legal maximum TAEG**,
and that ceiling is **lower for purpose-stated loans** (education, health, renewables, equipment
leasing) than for general-purpose ones — so lenders market dedicated purpose products and compete
on *fees, speed, insurance, and the headline installment* more than on raw rate. Because the
numbers move every quarter and line-ups churn, this brief maps the **structure**; the figures are
the refresh pass.

## 4. Cross-cutting themes

1. **Pricing converges toward the applicable TAEG cap.** Because BdP caps maximum TAEG per
   category (Brief 02 §2), headline rates cluster just under the ceiling — competition shifts to
   *fees, speed, insurance, and the headline installment* rather than raw rate.
2. **Finalidade as a pricing lever.** The **lower** cap for *educação / saúde / energias renováveis
   / locação financeira de equipamentos* means lenders market dedicated purpose products and
   borrowers can access cheaper credit by declaring an eligible purpose — a structural feature with
   no card analogue.
3. **Consolidação as a growth product.** *Crédito consolidado* (Brief 01 §3) is a recurring
   specialist play — sold as "one lower installment," it stretches the term and so often *raises*
   total interest even as the monthly payment falls; an honest comparison must surface the MTIC.
4. **Online / instant approval is the new battleground.** Younited, the digital arms (ActivoBank,
   Openbank) and specialists compete on *simulador*-to-disbursement speed; incumbents follow.
5. **Insurance attach is core specialist margin.** *Seguro de proteção ao crédito* is cross-sold
   hard by the specialists — it materially affects the real cost and must be read inside the TAEG.
6. **The stamp-duty wedge.** *Imposto do selo* (Brief 02 §2) — the one-off selo on principal plus
   ~4% on interest/fees plus the 50% surcharge — is a **tax, which DL 133/2009 excludes from the
   TAEG**, so it sits **outside** both the TAN and the TAEG. The **true cost (MTIC) therefore
   exceeds what either the TAN or the TAEG implies**; any fair cost comparison surfaces it.
7. **Two lending models compete.** **Balance-sheet banks** (deposit-funded, relationship-led) vs
   **ASFAC-style specialists** (lending-led, POS/online, insurance-rich) — different economics, same
   capped product.
