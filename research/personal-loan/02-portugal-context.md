## In plain English

A Portuguese crédito pessoal works like the generic amortizing loan in Brief 01, but local rules
bend it in one way that has no equivalent in most countries: **the maximum legal cost of the loan
depends on what you say the money is for.** Banco de Portugal publishes a *ceiling* on the all-in
annual cost (the maximum TAEG) every quarter — and crédito pessoal is split into two buckets with
**different ceilings**: a **lower** cap for loans earmarked for **education, health, renewable
energy, or equipment leasing**, and a **higher** cap for a general-purpose loan with **no stated
purpose**. Because lenders price up to the ceiling, **declaring an eligible purpose can make the
very same loan cheaper** — the single most important Portugal-specific fact about this product.

Two more local forces matter. **The state taxes the borrowing itself** through *imposto do selo*
(stamp duty) — but for a fixed-term personal loan it works differently than for a card: it's a
**one-off duty on the amount borrowed, scaled by how long the loan runs**, rather than a monthly
charge on a revolving balance. And **early repayment is a right, not a favour**: you can pay the
loan off ahead of time, and the law **caps** what the lender may charge you for doing so.

On top of all that sit the consumer-protection rails Portugal shares across all consumer credit —
a mandatory affordability check against the central credit register (**CRC**), a standardised
pre-contract sheet (**FIN**), a 14-day cooling-off right, and the **PARI/PERSI** procedures that
must run before a lender can pursue someone who falls behind.

## 1. Portuguese vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Crédito pessoal* | Personal loan — fixed amount, fixed term, amortizing, unsecured |
| *Crédito aos consumidores* | Consumer credit (the umbrella regime; crédito pessoal is one category) |
| *Finalidade* | The declared **purpose** of the loan — drives the applicable rate cap |
| *Montante* / *prazo* | Loan amount / term |
| *Prestação* | The (usually monthly) installment |
| **TAN** (*taxa anual nominal*) | Nominal annual interest rate |
| **TAEG** (*taxa anual de encargos efetiva global*) | All-in effective annual cost (the PT/EU APR) — interest + fees |
| **MTIC** (*montante total imputado ao consumidor*) | Total cost of the credit to the consumer (all euros paid) |
| *Comissão de abertura / dossier* | One-off origination fee charged at disbursement |
| *Reembolso antecipado* | Early repayment (partial or total) |
| *Crédito consolidado* | Debt-consolidation loan (rolls several debts into one) |
| *Fiador / avalista* | Guarantor — third party liable on default |
| *Seguro de proteção ao crédito* | Payment-protection / credit-life insurance, often cross-sold |

## 2. Regulatory framework

**Supervisor.** *Banco de Portugal* (BdP) supervises retail-banking conduct, including consumer
credit; crédito pessoal sits squarely inside the *crédito aos consumidores* regime.

**Consumer-credit regime — Decreto-Lei n.º 133/2009** (transposing the EU Consumer Credit
Directive 2008/48/EC). For an amortizing personal loan it requires:
- a mandatory **avaliação de solvabilidade** (creditworthiness/affordability assessment);
- delivery of the **FIN** (*Ficha de Informação Normalizada* — the PT form of the EU SECCI),
  the standardised pre-contractual sheet that makes offers comparable;
- a **14-day right of free withdrawal** (*livre revogação*) after signing;
- the right of **early repayment** (*reembolso antecipado*) at any time — see below.

**★ The defining PT feature — purpose-segmented maximum-TAEG caps.** Under DL 133/2009 (art. 28),
BdP publishes **quarterly maximum TAEGs by credit category**, and **crédito pessoal is itself
split by purpose** into (at least) two distinct buckets:
- **"Crédito pessoal — Finalidade: Educação, Saúde, Energias renováveis e Locação financeira de
  equipamentos"** → a **LOWER** maximum TAEG; and
- **"Crédito pessoal — Outros créditos pessoais (sem finalidade específica)"** → a **HIGHER**
  maximum TAEG.

  These sit alongside the *crédito automóvel* buckets and the *cartões de crédito / linhas de
  crédito / contas correntes / facilidades de descoberto* + *crédito revolving* buckets (the
  latter referenced in the [card brief](../credit-cards/02-portugal-context.md) §2). The cap
  **mechanism** (verify the exact multipliers): a category's maximum TAEG may not exceed the
  **previous quarter's average TAEG for that category by more than ~25% (×1.25)**, and may not
  exceed the **overall consumer-credit average by more than ~50% (×1.5)** — the binding cap is the
  lower result. **Consequences specific to crédito pessoal:** (1) pricing **clusters just under
  the applicable cap**; (2) because the purpose-stated bucket has a **lower** ceiling, **declaring
  an eligible *finalidade* (education / health / renewables / equipment leasing) can lower the
  legal — and therefore the marketed — cost of the same loan**; this makes *finalidade* a genuine
  pricing lever, not just a label. `[REFRESH]` the **current-quarter** maximum TAEGs **per
  category** and confirm the ×1.25 / ×1.5 multipliers and the exact category labels.

**Early-repayment compensation — capped (a key personal-loan specific).** The borrower may repay
early at any time; the lender may claim a **capped compensation** for lost interest, calculated on
the **capital repaid**:
- historically **≈0.5%** of the capital repaid if **more than 1 year** of the term remains, and
- **≈0.25%** if **1 year or less** remains,
- with the compensation **not exceeding the interest the borrower would have paid** over the
  remaining period, and certain repayments exempt. `[REFRESH]` the exact percentages, the >1yr /
  ≤1yr threshold, and current exemptions against the live DL 133/2009 text.

**Imposto do Selo (stamp duty) — different shape than for a card.** Credit-utilization duty falls
under **Verba 17.1** of the *Tabela Geral do Imposto do Selo*, whose sub-items split by maturity —
so a **fixed-term** crédito pessoal attracts a **one-off** duty on the **amount of credit
granted**, scaled by term:
- **Verba 17.1.1 — term < 1 year:** a per-**month** rate (base ≈ **0.04%/month** of the credit
  used). **Verba 17.1.2 — term 1 to 5 years:** a flat ≈ **0.50%** of the principal. **Verba 17.1.3
  — term ≥ 5 years:** a flat ≈ **0.60%**. These are the **base TGIS rates** — `[REFRESH]` the exact
  current figures and confirm the consumer-credit schedule.
- **Consumer-credit surcharge (*agravamento*):** a **50% increase** on consumer-credit stamp duty
  has applied in recent years, lifting the base bands to roughly **0.06%/month**, **0.75%**, and
  **0.90%** respectively. `[REFRESH]` whether still in force and the resulting effective rates.
- This **contrasts** with the *revolving / undetermined-term* case (**Verba 17.1.4**, the card
  mechanism), a **recurring monthly** duty on the average debit balance (base ≈ 0.04%/month →
  ≈ 0.06% surcharged). *Note:* the sibling [card brief](../credit-cards/02-portugal-context.md)
  cites ~0.128%/month and "Verba 17.2"; both the **rate** and the **verba number** there should be
  reconciled against Verba 17.1.4 in that brief's own refresh. `[REFRESH]`.
- **Verba 17.3 — interest & fees:** **~4%** stamp duty on interest charged and on bank commissions
  (e.g. the *comissão de abertura*). `[REFRESH]`.
- Net effect: **imposto do selo is a *tax*, and DL 133/2009 excludes taxes from the TAEG** — so the
  one-off selo on the principal, the ~4% on interest/fees, and the 50% surcharge sit **outside**
  both the TAN and the TAEG. The **true cost (MTIC) therefore exceeds what either the TAN or the
  TAEG implies** — the essential point for any honest cost comparison.

**Central de Responsabilidades de Crédito (CRC).** BdP's **central credit register** — lenders
must report exposures and **consult it during the solvency assessment**, so existing debts (and any
defaults) directly shape approval and pricing. (Same register as the card brief §2.)

**Default-handling regimes — Decreto-Lei n.º 227/2012 (PARI / PERSI).** Before a lender may pursue
legal action against a borrower in arrears it must run **PARI** (a pre-default action plan) and
**PERSI** (a mandatory out-of-court resolution procedure) — inserting extra, borrower-protective
steps into the §5 default lifecycle. Identical machinery to the [card brief](../credit-cards/02-portugal-context.md) §2;
for crédito pessoal it bites on the missed-installment path.

**Upcoming — CCD2 (Directive (EU) 2023/2225).** Repeals 2008/48 with tighter creditworthiness and
wider scope; national transposition/application lands around **2025–2026**. `[REFRESH]` the exact
PT transposition status and dates — a live moving target.

**Other shared rails:** *avaliação de solvabilidade* duties, **RGPD/GDPR**, and BdP rules on
*comissões* transparency (the published *preçário*).

## 3. Market characteristics — "why Portugal is different"

- **A two-tribe supply side.** Crédito pessoal is offered both by **balance-sheet banks** (as one
  product in a full range) and by **consumer-finance specialists** — many grouped under **ASFAC**
  (*Associação de Instituições de Crédito Especializado*), whose members live and breathe consumer
  credit and originate heavily at point-of-sale and online. `[REFRESH]` the banks-vs-specialists
  split — *the single most decision-relevant sizing figure.*
- **Purpose-driven pricing.** Because of the per-*finalidade* caps (§2), the market visibly prices
  **education / health / renewables / equipment** loans below general-purpose ones — and lenders
  market dedicated "crédito [finalidade]" products to occupy the lower-cap bucket.
- **Consolidation is a recurring demand cycle.** *Crédito consolidado* surges when households are
  stretched — packaged as "one lower installment," it is a major specialist product.
- **Online/instant origination is the growth front.** *Simuladores* and near-instant decisioning
  (specialists and digital banks) are reshaping how the product is sold.
- `[REFRESH]` total consumer-credit volumes, crédito pessoal's share, average TAEG by category,
  typical amount/term bands (BdP *Custo do crédito aos consumidores* / retail-banking reports; ASFAC).

## 4. PT-adapted lifecycle (delta vs. Brief 01 §5)

Application (often via **online *simulador***) → **KYC/AML** (Lei 83/2017) → **mandatory
*avaliação de solvabilidade*** with **CRC consultation** + income evidence → decision + pricing
**(TAN/TAEG bounded by the per-*finalidade* cap)** → **FIN** delivered pre-contract → contract +
**14-day *livre revogação*** → **lump-sum disbursement** → repayment in **prestações via débito
direto** → servicing → **reembolso antecipado** *(with the capped compensation of §2)* → maturity
& closure, **or on default: PARI → PERSI** *before* any legal action or CRC default marking →
recovery / write-off.

The bolded steps — the **per-purpose-capped pricing**, the solvency assessment + CRC, the FIN and
withdrawal window, the **capped early repayment**, and the **PARI/PERSI gateway** — are the
Portugal-specific insertions into the generic amortizing-loan lifecycle.
